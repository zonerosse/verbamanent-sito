A meeting without minutes is a meeting that, a week later, nobody remembers the same way. Minutes record **what was decided, who has to do what and by when**. Done well, they take two minutes to read and save you the next meeting.

Here's what they must include, a complete example and a blank template to copy.

## What meeting minutes should include

| Section | What to write |
|---|---|
| **Header** | Meeting title, date, start and end time, place or platform (Meet, Teams, Zoom) |
| **Attendees** | Present, apologies, who chaired and who took the minutes |
| **Agenda** | The list of topics, numbered |
| **Discussion summary** | For each item, the main positions in a few lines, not everyone's words |
| **Decisions** | What was decided, written so it can't be misread |
| **Action items** | Who does what and by when |
| **Next meeting** | Date, time and topics already planned |

The golden rule: **decisions and action items are the most important part**. That's usually all readers look for.

## Blank template to copy

Copy this outline into a document and fill it in during or right after the meeting.

<pre class="modello">MEETING MINUTES: [title]

Date: [day] · Time: [start]–[end] · Place: [room or platform]

ATTENDEES
Present: [names] — chair: [name] — minutes: [name]
Apologies: [names]

AGENDA
1. [topic]
2. [topic]
3. Any other business

DISCUSSION AND DECISIONS
1. [topic] — Summary: [2–3 lines]. Decision: [what was decided].
2. [topic] — Summary: [2–3 lines]. Decision: [what was decided].

ACTION ITEMS
- [who] — [what] — by [date]
- [who] — [what] — by [date]

NEXT MEETING
[date, time, place] — Topics: [list]</pre>

## How to write good minutes: practical tips

- **Write them within 24 hours**, while the meeting is still fresh. Minutes that arrive a week later go unread.
- **Decisions, not chatter**: you don't need to record who said what word for word, just the main positions and the outcome.
- **Every action has a name and a date**. "We need to call the supplier" isn't an action; "Mark calls the supplier by Friday" is.
- **Short, clear sentences**: someone who wasn't there should understand everything without asking.
- **Send them to everyone**, including those who were absent, and ask for corrections by a set date.
- **Start from the minutes** at the next meeting: the first thing to do is check last time's action items.

## Formal minutes: when there are strict rules

Minutes of a work meeting between colleagues are free-form: they just need to be clear. Some meetings, however, have minutes required by law or by the organisation's rules, for example **board meetings**, **shareholders' meetings** and **condominium or homeowners' association meetings**. There, quorums, votes, signatures and record-keeping also matter, and it's best to follow the guidance of your company secretary, property manager or a professional.
