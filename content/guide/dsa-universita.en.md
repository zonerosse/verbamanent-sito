At secondary school there was a personalised plan written by your teachers. At university there's no class council and nobody comes looking for you: **you have to ask** for what you're entitled to. The good news is that in Italy Law 170/2010 applies here too, and every university has a dedicated service.

This guide covers what a student with dyslexia or another specific learning difficulty is entitled to at an Italian university, which diagnosis you need, how admission tests and exams work and the steps to make your request. For the bigger picture, see the guide to [assistive tools and exemptions](/en/guides/assistive-tools-dyslexia/).

## What the rules say

The national guidelines of 2011 (paragraph 6.7, on universities) ask universities to provide:

| What | In practice |
|---|---|
| **Extra time in exams** | Up to a maximum of 30% more, or fewer exercises with the same objectives |
| **Tools already used at school** | Lecture recording, digital texts, text-to-speech, a computer with a spellchecker |
| **Oral exams** | Preferred over written ones, taking the student's profile into account |
| **Admission tests** | Extra time, no more than 30% |

Each university turns these rules into its own **regulations**: who to contact, which forms, which deadlines. That's why you should read your university's.

## The diagnosis you need

To get the measures you need a **diagnosis (certification) of an SpLD** issued by the national health service or by specialists and centres accredited by the Region.

| Your diagnosis | Usually |
|---|---|
| Made **as an adult** | Valid for your whole university course |
| Made **as a minor** | Must be updated if it's more than three years old |
| Made by a **non-accredited** centre | May not be accepted: ask your university's service |

The exact rules vary by region and by university: your university's SpLD service will tell you whether your diagnosis is fine as it is.

## Admission tests

For limited-enrolment courses and entry tests (such as TOLC), students with SpLDs normally get **30% extra time**, often a separate room and a non-scientific calculator. Maps, formula sheets and dictionaries are usually **not** allowed.

What counts is the **test notice**: it says what to request, by when and with which documents. The deadline for requesting measures is often earlier than the test registration deadline, so act early.

## Exams

Once you're enrolled, the measures for exams are agreed with the SpLD service. The most common:

- **extra time**, up to 30%, or **fewer questions** with the same objectives;
- **text-to-speech** and a **computer with a spellchecker** in written exams;
- **concept maps and formula sheets**, if provided for and agreed with the lecturer;
- an **oral exam** instead of a written one, when possible;
- marking **content**, not spelling.

In many universities the service prepares a document listing your measures and sends it to your lecturers. But the most useful step is yours: **write to the lecturer before the exam**, introduce yourself and confirm how the exam will be run.

## University services

Besides exam measures, many universities offer:

- **peer tutors**: older students who help with note-taking, exam preparation, and reading and writing during exams;
- **study-skills counselling**;
- **materials in digital format**;
- at some universities, **fee exemption** for students with a certified SpLD (Sapienza University of Rome, for example, waives tuition fees for bachelor's and master's degrees).

## The steps, in order

1. **Find your university's SpLD service**: it's usually called "Disability and SpLD service" or "Inclusion".
2. **Check your diagnosis**: ask whether it's fine or needs updating.
3. **Register with the service** with your diagnosis, as soon as you enrol (or before the admission test).
4. **Request the measures** for tests, exams and tutors, meeting the deadlines.
5. **Contact your lecturers** before each exam.
6. **Renew your request** if your university requires it every year.

## Studying at university with dyslexia

Exam measures help, but your method during the semester makes the difference:

- **record your lectures** (it's an allowed tool, but tell the lecturer) and listen again instead of rereading pages of notes;
- **one map per lecture**, made the same day: by the end of the course you already have your review material. Here's [how to make concept maps](/en/guides/concept-maps-dyslexia/);
- **review at intervals**, a little every day, instead of everything the week before;
- **practise with exam timing**, including the 30% extra: knowing how much time you have per question reduces anxiety.

{{< vm-nota >}}Dyslexia mode records the lecture and prepares easy-to-read notes, **karaoke** with the lecturer's real voice and the lecture map. Before the exam there are **multiple-choice tests with 30% extra time**, **oral exam practice** and **dictated written answers**, marked on content and never on spelling. You'll find it all on the [Dyslexia](/en/dyslexia/) page.{{< /vm-nota >}}

## The most common mistakes

- Waiting until the first exam to contact the SpLD service.
- Assuming your secondary-school diagnosis is fine.
- Not reading the admission test notice, and missing the deadline for measures.
- Turning up to an exam without telling the lecturer.
- Giving up the measures for fear of being judged: they're a right, and lecturers are used to them.
